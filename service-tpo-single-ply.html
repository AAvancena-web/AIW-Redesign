Thermoplastic Polyolefin

TPO Single Ply Roofing in Auckland

Thermoplastic polyolefin is a thin sheet that is hot air welded at the seams rather than torched. It is light, it reflects heat rather than absorbing it, and it is free of the toxic plasticisers older single ply systems relied on.

The seams are the selling point. A hot air weld fuses the two sheets into one piece of material, with no solvent, no adhesive and nothing at the lap that can dry out or creep over time. The weld can be tested along its length before the roof is signed off.

TPO earns its place on large, reasonably simple roof areas, and on roofs where weight is a constraint. The reflective surface also keeps the space below cooler, which matters on a warehouse or a north facing commercial roof far more than the brochure suggests.

How We Do It

Welded, Not Glued

What the welding rig buys you over an adhered lap.

The seams

One Piece of Material

Hot air fuses the sheets together, so the lap is as strong as the membrane rather than as strong as the glue.

  • No solvents or adhesives at the laps
  • Welds probed and checked before sign off
  • Nothing at the lap that can dry out or creep
  • Detailing welded the same way as the field
The surface

Reflective and Recyclable

A white or light grey surface sends heat back rather than storing it, and the material can be recycled at end of life.

  • Reflects rather than absorbs solar gain
  • Keeps the space below measurably cooler
  • Free of toxic plasticisers
  • Recyclable at the end of its service life

Commonly Asked

TPO Single Ply, Answered Straight

The questions we get asked most about this system. There are more on the FAQ page.

What is TPO single ply, and when would you use it?

A thin thermoplastic sheet that is hot air welded at the seams rather than torched. It is light, it reflects heat rather than absorbing it, and it suits large simple roof areas where the weight of a bitumen system is a problem.

How are TPO seams joined?

With hot air, which fuses the two sheets into a single piece of material. There is no solvent and no adhesive at the lap, and the weld can be probed and checked along its length before the roof is handed over.

Is TPO better than torch on?

Neither is better in general. TPO wins on weight, speed over large areas and heat reflection. Torch on wins on durability, lap width and awkward detailing. We will tell you which one we would put on your roof and why.

Can TPO be recycled?

Yes, the material is recyclable at the end of its service life, and unlike some older single ply systems it contains no toxic plasticisers.
