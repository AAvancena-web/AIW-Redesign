Project Overview

Summerset St Johns

Around 2000 square metres of Rooflogic Fibertite Xtreme membrane to roof and internal gutter areas, delivered against a tight programme and a run of difficult weather.

Auckland Integrity Waterproofing completed approximately 2000 square metres of Rooflogic Fibertite Xtreme membrane system to the roof and internal gutter areas.

The project presented several key challenges, including tight programme deadlines, adverse weather conditions and design coordination issues.

On This Job

What Had to Be Solved

Every roof has something. These are the ones that shaped how this job was run.

The Challenge

Weather Against the Programme

Adverse weather placed additional pressure on the programme and cut the time available for external installation works.

To maintain productivity and minimise delays, the team shifted work into temporary shrink wrap areas, so installation could continue under cover. That kept onsite productivity up and the job moving towards its deadline.

The Challenge

Details Missing From the Design

Several design coordination issues and missing construction details were identified in the early stages of the project.

These were flagged promptly with the design team to allow time for review, design development, approval and compliance, while accounting for the lead times on material fabrication and procurement.

The Challenge

Resolved With the Design Team and Rooflogic

Close collaboration with the design team and Rooflogic was essential in resolving these issues and making sure the proposed solutions met the technical and compliance standards required.

Early identification and proactive coordination kept the programme impact small and let the installation proceed in line with the approved design.

What Went Onto This Roof

Roof and gutters

Fibertite Xtreme, Single Layer

A single layer Fibertite membrane over cover board, taken across both the roof areas and the internal gutters.

  • Approximately 2000 square metres in total
  • Roof areas and internal gutters in one system
  • PIR and Stonewall insulation beneath
  • Cover board as the membrane substrate
Working under cover

Shrink Wrap Where It Was Needed

Temporary shrink wrap areas let the crews keep installing while the weather was against the programme.

  • Work moved under cover rather than stopping
  • Protection from weather during installation
  • Productivity maintained through poor conditions
  • Programme deadline kept in sight
