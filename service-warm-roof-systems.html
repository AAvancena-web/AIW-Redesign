Insulation and Membrane in One

Warm Roof Systems in Auckland

Warm roofs combine waterproofing and insulation into one high performance roof build up, so the thermal layer and the weather layer are designed together rather than bolted to each other.

Unlike a traditional roof, where the insulation is installed below the roof structure, a warm roof places the insulation above the structural roof deck and directly below the waterproofing membrane. That creates a continuous layer of insulation across the roof, and it keeps the roof structure itself warmer and drier.

Auckland Integrity Waterproofing installs warm roof systems for residential and commercial projects throughout Auckland, including new construction, reroofing and roof upgrades.

The Case For It

Why Choose a Warm Roof?

A properly designed warm roof can provide several advantages.

Improved thermal performance and energy efficiency
Continuous insulation with reduced thermal bridging
Reduced risk of condensation within the roof structure
Improved internal comfort throughout the year
Suitable for new builds and reroofing projects
Compatible with a range of membrane roofing systems
Tapered insulation can be used to create or improve roof falls

Layer by Layer

The Warm Roof Build Up

A typical PIR insulated warm roof, from the structural deck out to the weather layer.

1

Structural Roof Deck

The deck the whole system is built on. Everything above it is designed around what it is and what it can carry.

2

Vapour Control Layer

Laid directly on the deck, to keep warm moist air from inside the building out of the insulation above it.

3

PIR Insulation

Rigid boards laid above the structure rather than below it, giving a continuous layer with reduced thermal bridging. Tapered boards where falls are needed.

4

Waterproofing Membrane

The weather layer over the insulation, either TPO single ply or a two layer torch on system depending on the design.

PIR rigid insulation is commonly used in warm roof systems because it provides a high level of thermal performance without requiring excessive roof thickness. We work with leading New Zealand membrane suppliers and can recommend a system to suit the building design, the required R value, the substrate, the roof falls and the expected service life.

How We Do It

PIR and Tapered Warm Roofs

Two decisions do most of the work on a warm roof: what the insulation is, and whether it also forms the falls.

PIR insulated

A Build Up That Earns Its Thickness

PIR rigid insulation gives a high level of thermal performance without requiring excessive roof thickness, which matters when upstands and thresholds are fixed.

  • Vapour control layer laid directly on the structural deck
  • Rigid PIR above the structure, not below it
  • Continuous insulation with reduced thermal bridging
  • Membrane laid straight over the insulation
Tapered

Falls Formed in the Insulation

Where additional falls are required, tapered PIR is designed into the warm roof rather than built into the deck or screeded on afterwards.

  • Water directed towards gutters, sumps and outlets
  • No additional screeds or changes to the structural deck
  • Insulation and drainage solved in the same layer
  • Suited to large commercial, concrete and reroof projects

Commonly Asked

Warm Roof Systems, Answered Straight

The questions we get asked most about this system. There are more on the FAQ page.

What is a warm roof?

A warm roof places the insulation above the structural roof deck and directly below the waterproofing membrane, rather than below the structure. That gives a continuous layer of insulation across the roof and keeps the roof structure itself warmer and drier.

Can a warm roof be added when reroofing?

Often, yes. Where the existing structure and roofing are suitable, adding insulation as part of the reroof is a chance to improve weathertightness and thermal performance at the same time. We will inspect the existing roof, assess the substrate and the drainage, and tell you whether it suits.

What is tapered insulation for?

Creating or improving roof falls. Instead of forming the entire fall in the structural deck or adding screeds, tapered PIR is designed to direct water towards gutters, sumps and outlets while also providing the insulation. It is particularly useful on large commercial roofs, concrete roofs and reroofs where the existing falls are inadequate.

Which membrane goes over a warm roof?

Either TPO single ply or a two layer torch on system, depending on the project. We work with leading New Zealand membrane suppliers and recommend a system to suit the building design, the required R value, the substrate, the roof falls and the expected service life.
